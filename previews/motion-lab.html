Staggered scroll reveal

Ship this

The single biggest upgrade. Every section's children fade up 12px as they enter the viewport, 60ms apart. It gives the page a sense of order and pacing without moving anything permanently. One IntersectionObserver, ~15 lines, no library.

02

Masked line reveal for the hero name

Ship this

The hero name rises out from behind an invisible mask, one line at a time. Pairs with the typewriter you already have, so the hero reads as one deliberate opening sequence instead of two unrelated tricks. Pure CSS once the spans exist; splitting is 3 lines of JS.

420ms · ease-emphasized · 90ms per line · overflow hidden mask · starts at 60ms

Gotcha. The mask line needs a little bottom padding or the tail of g, y, p gets shaved off. Also set overflow:hidden on the outer, never the inner, or the text clips on hover.

03

Scroll-linked reading progress rail

Ship this

A 2px accent line pinned to the left edge that fills as you scroll the page. It gives a recruiter a sense of how much is left, and it is the one scroll effect that is genuinely zero JavaScript. Pairs naturally with the sticky section rail you already have.

animation-timeline: scroll(root block) · 2px wide · transform scaleY · no JS at all

Scroll the page and watch the bar on your screen edgepure CSS

Look at the far left edge of the browser window. There is a thin vertical blue line filling from top to bottom as you scroll. It is position:fixed plus animation-timeline: scroll(root block) and nothing else.

The rule that trips everyone. animation-timeline must be declared after any animation shorthand, because the shorthand silently resets it back to auto. And animation-duration: 1ms is required or Firefox ignores the whole thing.

Ship this

Your experience section already has a vertical gradient line. Scroll-linking its scaleY to the section's own view progress means the line paints downward exactly as the card arrives. It communicates sequence, which is the one thing motion should do here.

05

Project thumbnail drifts on scroll

Use sparingly

As a project card passes through the viewport its screenshot scales from 1.04 down to 1.0 and the gradient veil thins. Subtle depth without a shadow. The restraint rule matters here: one scroll-linked element per viewport, never three stacked.
